## Agent notes (no thresholds changed)

- **TOST.** Equivalence to zero is not robust:
  - pythia-1.4b passes with the model SE but not with the relation bootstrap; the bootstrap 90% CI upper bound is 0.0208, just above the 0.02 margin.
  - pythia-2.8b fails both versions.
  - The bootstrap CI is the more conservative of the two because it accounts for clustering by relation.
- **Minimal pairs.** The only small p is pythia-2.8b `one_per_object_token` (N = 39 pairs, p 0.0046). It is one of 6 uncorrected tests in an exploratory analysis, and the larger pythia-2.8b pair sets point the same way, but weakly: `all_pairs` p 0.7164 and `same_relation` p 0.6152. Treat it as a lead to check, not a finding.
- **SANSKRITI.** Of 900 rows, 836 are censored and only 64 resolved, and 77% of items have zero Pile co-occurrence. The slice is too sparse to test the frequency effect.
- **LRE faithfulness.** Faithfulness is 0.496 overall but 0.138 on facts the model gets right, and in 0.486 of test facts the model's top token is its relation's most common one. The overall number is therefore driven largely by facts where the model predicts the same token every time. The p for log cooc given faithfulness is MISSING because the fixed-effect SE was not finite: the fit ended at a boundary, which is the source of the statsmodels sqrt warnings in `results/s4_cpu.out`.
- **Transfer models.** MISSING (needs a GPU). S4 transfer is therefore not tested.
